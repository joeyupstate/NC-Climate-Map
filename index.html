About this map

How it works

What it's for

Find the plant hardiness zone for any spot in North Carolina based on recent winters, not decades-old averages, and see which palms you can grow there.

How to use it

  1. Pick a time window: the past 20, 15, 10, 8, 5 or 2 years, or just last winter. The whole map recolors.
  2. Tap a town or any spot on the map. Rural areas work too.
  3. Read the results: the zone, the coldest night on record for that window, and two tabs: Palms (what will grow) and Climate (the coldest night of every winter).

How the zones are calculated

For every spot, we find the coldest night of each winter (July to June), then average those lows over the window you pick. That average sets the zone, using the same 5°F half-zones and the same colors as the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map.

The official USDA map averages 30 years (1991–2020). Shorter windows here show how recent winters compare. A run of mild winters, or one brutal freeze, changes the picture.

Palm categories

  • Bulletproof Grows without protection. Even a bad winter for the zone won't kill it.
  • Marginal Usually fine, but a bad winter may kill it if it's unprotected.
  • Needs protection Wrap, cover or heat it most winters.

Categories come from each palm's cold tolerance, adjusted by grower experience.

Where the data comes from

  • Temperatures: PRISM (Oregon State University) daily low temperatures on an 800 m grid, the same source behind the USDA map.
  • Towns & counties: US Census Bureau boundaries.
  • Elevations: USGS 3D Elevation Program.

Checked against 182 NC weather stations, our 20-winter averages are within about 1°F of what thermometers recorded.

Keep in mind

Zones are a guide, not a guarantee. Your own yard can be warmer or colder: frost pockets, south-facing walls, city heat and water all matter. So do how long a freeze lasts, wet cold, and a plant's size and age. If a spot sits near a zone line, plan for the colder zone.
